Clean Room in Pharmaceuticals

Controlled environments engineered to maintain ultra-low levels of particulate contamination, ensuring the safety and quality of pharmaceutical products.

What is a Clean Room?

🏭

Definition

A clean room is a controlled environment where the concentration of airborne particles is controlled to specified limits. These rooms are used in pharmaceutical manufacturing, especially for sterile product production, to prevent contamination of drugs during processing, filling, and packaging.

Clean Room Classifications (EU GMP)

Grade A

Critical Zone

High-risk operations such as filling, stopper bowls, open ampoules/vials, and aseptic connections. Laminar airflow conditions required.

Background Environment

Background environment for Grade A zone for aseptic preparation and filling. Serves as the immediate surrounding area.

Grade C

Less Critical Steps

Used for carrying out less critical stages in the manufacturing of sterile products, such as preparation of solutions to be filtered.

Non-Sterile Steps

Clean area for carrying out less critical stages such as component handling after washing, or for non-sterile product manufacturing.

Key Equipment & Components

🔲

HEPA / ULPA Filters

High Efficiency Particulate Air filters capture 99.97% of particles ≥0.3µm. ULPA filters capture 99.999% of particles ≥0.12µm. Installed in ceiling for unidirectional laminar airflow.

🌬️

HVAC System

Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning system provides controlled temperature, humidity, and air changes. Maintains 20+ air changes per hour in critical zones.

🚿

Air Shower

Personnel decontamination chamber using high-velocity HEPA-filtered air jets to remove loose particles from garments before entering clean areas.

🔒

Airlocks / Interlocking Doors

Pass-through chambers with interlocking doors that prevent direct connection between rooms of different grades, maintaining pressure differentials.

🧪

Laminar Air Flow (LAF) Units

Provide Grade A environment with unidirectional HEPA-filtered airflow at 0.36-0.54 m/s velocity for critical operations like aseptic filling.

📡

Particle Counter

Continuous monitoring device that counts airborne particles of sizes ≥0.5µm and ≥5.0µm per cubic meter to verify clean room classification compliance.

📋

BMS (Building Management System)

Automated system for real-time monitoring and control of temperature, humidity, pressure differentials, and alarm management with data logging.

🧫

Settle Plates & Active Air Samplers

Used for microbial environmental monitoring. Settle plates (passive) expose agar to air; active samplers draw measured air volumes onto agar surfaces.

Regulatory Guidelines

🇪🇺 EU GMP Annex 1

Manufacture of Sterile Medicinal Products — Primary guideline for clean room classification (Grade A, B, C, D) in the EU. Revised 2022.

🇺🇸 FDA 21 CFR Parts 210 & 211

Current Good Manufacturing Practice for Finished Pharmaceuticals — US FDA requirements for manufacturing facility controls.

🌐 ISO 14644

International standard for clean rooms and controlled environments. ISO Classes 1-9 based on particle concentration limits.

🇺🇸 USP <797> & <800>

Standards for pharmaceutical compounding — sterile preparations and handling of hazardous drugs, respectively.

🇮🇳 Schedule M (India)

Good Manufacturing Practices under Drugs and Cosmetics Act — defines clean room requirements for Indian pharmaceutical manufacturers.

🌍 WHO TRS (Technical Report Series)

WHO guidelines on GMP for pharmaceutical products — provides international benchmark standards for clean room operations.

✅ Do's in Clean Room

  • Follow gowning procedure strictly
  • Report any equipment malfunction
  • Keep movements slow and deliberate
  • Sanitize hands frequently
  • Enter through designated airlocks only
  • Follow cleaning SOPs meticulously

❌ Don'ts in Clean Room

  • No cosmetics or jewelry
  • No food or beverages
  • No running or rapid movements
  • No cardboard or wooden pallets
  • No unauthorized personnel
  • No leaning on walls or equipment
